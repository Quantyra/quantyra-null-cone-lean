## Possible acceleration

The [Brenier BoundaryStrip module](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/OAI/Analysis/Brenier/BoundaryStrip.lean) provides one-dimensional translated convex-strip volume bounds and a product-measure estimate via Fubini. This is a concrete pattern for future continuum measure arguments, although our uniform-marginal rectangle estimate may be simpler directly in mathlib. The [DensityCDF module](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/OAI/Analysis/Brenier/DensityCDF.lean) develops cumulative integrals, order isomorphisms and quantiles for positive continuous interval densities. That could help a later extension dropping the uniform-marginal gauge; it is not a two-dimensional density supremum reconstruction theorem.

The [Brenier comparator configuration](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/ComparatorChallenges/Brenier.json) separates a challenge statement, solution and definitions, and permits only propext, Quot.sound and Classical.choice. A similar separation would strengthen auditability of our theorem's encoded statement. Our existing axiom audit already checks those same permitted dependencies. The released toolchain is Lean 4.34.1; ours is pinned to 4.30.0. Reuse needs a compatibility check and narrowly scoped ports, not a wholesale dependency import. Comparator checks are not claimed as run here.

No inspected module supplied our remaining iid grid-concentration bridge. This release has not shortened a measured proof-development time or improved our proved numerical estimate.

A separate author inference from OUR existing probability estimates is a potentially faster mathematical route: choosing grid scale approximately sqrt(log(n)/n), rather than n^(-1/4), appears compatible with both the occupancy union bound and the Hoeffding grid bound. Keeping the same deterministic reconstruction and cubic interpolation would suggest a coefficient term of order (log(n)/n)^(1/6). This is not derived from an OpenAI release result, and it is not promoted to a proved replacement bound: integer rounding, constants, threshold and complete derivation still need checking.

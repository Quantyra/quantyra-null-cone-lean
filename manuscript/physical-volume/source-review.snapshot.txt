## Reviewed primary proofs

### Imbens and Manski

Read the [2003 CeMMAP working paper CWP09/03](https://cemmap.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/CWP0903.pdf), dated May 29: sections 2–4.2, Lemma 3.3, Assumptions 4.1–4.4, and the appendix proof chain through Lemmas 7.1–7.7 and Lemma 4.1. Coverage uses uniform normal approximation, consistent variance estimates and special control of estimated identification width. Lemma 7.7 supplies one-sided critical-value control near zero width. The missing-data example observes selection indicators for the generated sample. S046 instead observes retained events conditional on n, so that propensity estimate is unavailable. The [2004 journal record](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2004.00555.x) was inspected, but the author-hosted journal PDF remains inaccessible; edition equivalence is unverified. The cited uniform-CLT reference was not independently audited. This comparison imports no theorem.

### Stoye

Read the [December 17, 2008 author preprint](https://stoye.economics.cornell.edu/docs/stoye-ecma2009-pre.pdf): sections 2–3, Assumptions 1–3, Lemmas 1–3, Propositions 1–3, Appendix A in full, and Appendix B's Gaussian formulas. The proofs separate shrinking-width sequences from widths large relative to sampling error. Local superefficiency supports the original interval; Proposition 3 uses width thresholding for the more general case. Variance floors and uniform normal approximation remain substantive assumptions. These are asymptotic coverage arguments, including near point identification, rather than finite binomial guarantees. The [2009 journal metadata](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA7347) and author publication listing were checked; the journal text was not compared line by line. A bounded correction search located no separate correction notice, which is not proof that none exists. S046 retains its finite statement uniform in n and R.

### Tudball and coauthors

Read [arXiv 1906.10159v4](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.10159v4), dated October 4, 2021: section 2, Appendices B–D, including Theorem 2.1 and its supporting proofs. Also inspected the [journal article](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article/110/2/485/6649721), sections 2–3, and its linked 11-page Supplementary Material, including Proposition 3, Lemmas 2–3 and Theorem 1's proof. The argument combines a relaxed feasible set, asymptotic normal calibration and a bound on failure of set containment. It requires optimizer and variance assumptions. The result remains asymptotic in the journal edition. Its ratio-weight optimization is a relevant comparison, but the general detector class in S046 has not been reduced to its finite-dimensional optimization hypotheses. S046 must establish its own finite joint guarantee; a pointwise constraint test alone does not discharge a simultaneous-set requirement. Invoked Shapiro results were identified, not independently re-proved. Journal publication was online in 2022 and in volume 110(2), 2023.

### Bounded selection probabilities

[Aronow and Lee, Biometrika 100(1), 2013, pages 235–240](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/ass064): Dan supplied the purchased six-page journal article and its two-page supplement in Downloads on 2026-10-09 (Hawaii). All pages and all four proofs are inspected. Proposition 1's appendix on pages 239–240 optimizes the normalized weighted mean, reduces to sorted endpoint weights and proves the threshold rule. Page 237 explicitly gives the same binary selection transforms used by our physical-volume report.

The supplement proves Lemma 1's equal-outcome weight property, Proposition 2's grouped-weight optimizer using inequalities (B1)–(B2), and Proposition 3's convergence under replicated finite-population sampling. The latter uses the strong law and strict threshold conditions to stabilize an optimal weight pattern. Lower counterparts follow by symmetry. Equal weight bounds are handled directly in our binary algebra; no strict-improvement argument is imported at R=1. The standard strong law was not separately re-proved for this source audit. These proofs provide no finite 95% confidence calibration or uniform geometric/full-observation minimax result.

## Consequences for the selected experiment

The following are decisions for our proof and comparison, not new conclusions attributed to the reviewed papers.

| Requirement | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Original density class K and fixed quarter-square interval | The retention transforms supply valid outer bounds. Their sharpness within K, given the entire marked-order law, is unproved. Do not identify their width with the minimax radius. |
| Full marked order | The upper estimator may discard relations and use the membership count. The lower bound must control the full observation law, or the stronger latent-coordinate experiment, before information is discarded. |
| Conditional retained sample | No generated-event count, rejection indicators, latent coordinates or calibrated clock may enter an estimator or comparator unless the experiment is explicitly changed. |
| Uniform R approaching 1 | Combine separate sample-noise and detector-confounding arguments with constants independent of R and n. A theorem for each fixed positive bias is insufficient. |
| Finite confidence | Retain exact binomial inversion or the accepted finite concentration bound. An asymptotic interval may be a labeled diagnostic, but does not replace a comparator with the specified finite guarantee. |
| Contribution | Any contribution must be stated at the level actually proved: geometric admissibility, full observation laws, finite joint bounds, executable calibration and formal certification. Neither generic partial identification nor bounded-weight mean estimation is a new topic here. |

This comparison does not establish a sharp identified set, shortest confidence interval, optimal monetary calibration policy, or physical sensor advantage. Those claims are outside the selected result. No new samples were generated and the S041 pilot remains frozen.

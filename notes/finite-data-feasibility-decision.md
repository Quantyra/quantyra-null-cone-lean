The reason to continue is the [degree-trimming construction](finite-data-interior-degree-rate.md). Its ordinary proof gives an order-only estimator in the full original K with 95% quotient sup-norm error at most `min(1/2,650(log n/n)^(1/4))`, for every n>=2. The polynomial exponents match, with a logarithmic gap and conservative upper constants. This is a substantial improvement over our earlier one-sixth-power estimator guarantee. It is not a proof of practical accuracy at n=3072 or of an exact minimax rate.

## What the result establishes

The fourth-root construction answers a rate question for one observed order, without assuming latent coordinates, stronger smoothness or independent overlapping suborders. One identity/transpose aligns the entire estimated square. The proof explicitly handles the boundary, finite confidence, integer floors, measurable selection and the flat-output branches. The lower bound concerns every estimator, rather than the tested LP alone.

The optional law-distance corollary is also derived in ordinary mathematics: for the actual n-point directed-order laws,

    d_conf <= min(1,1300(log n/n)^(1/4)+(10/9) TV), n>=2.

This compares two laws; it does not estimate their TV distance from a single observation. Neither published paper is changed by this follow-up.

The candidate contribution is the finite full-K reduction from order, especially its observable boundary filter and complete probability/error assembly. The familiar two-dimensional density-estimation exponent and standard two-point testing method are not claimed as discoveries. The lower bound is a benchmark supporting the improved upper rate, not by itself a reason for another paper.

## Boundaries and deferred work

The practical gate remains separate: useful full-square density bands must beat the width-one no-data comparator. A smaller reported error than the legacy baseline is insufficient if it merely recovers the flat estimator's deterministic radius 1/2. Empirical coverage of wide bands does not demonstrate informative reconstruction or prove uniform coverage over K. An LP relaxation obstruction is not an information-theoretic impossibility.

The new theorem's constants do not establish useful accuracy at the pilot sizes. It changes the boundary treatment and therefore needs its own scope before implementation or further experiments. Increasing n, changing the mesh, adding an oracle, or generating confirmation samples cannot be used to conceal the frozen method's outcome.

The remaining logarithmic gap would require a sharper lower bound, such as an admissible multi-alternative construction, or a sharper upper result. The present two-point argument does not resolve it. That extension, practical constant reduction, noisy observations and higher-dimensional reconstruction remain outside S031. The existing 2+1 coordinate-gauge obstruction continues to rule out the naive extension without an appropriate quotient and stability statement.
